Brief

Two sets of buttons, each expressing a tone. Tones can repeat. Every design has to do its job. Each one gets a drawing and a working prototype.

Tone is the soft attribute: the personality, the associations, the attitude. Function is the hard one: what the button is for and what it lets you do. A button's function is to say plainly that something will happen when you press it, and how much that thing matters next to the buttons around it. So the tone has to live in the shape, the fill and the way the button answers the pointer, while the order of importance still reads at a glance.

The rule that shapes everything: at least three button styles, each a different level of emphasis, built on one structure.

The brief shows two drawings. Apple's: a dark alert with a short title and a one-sentence message, then three full-width capsules stacked: a blue filled Primary, a grey Destructive with red text, and a grey Secondary with white text. Material's: a phone with two travel cards. The first card ends in a filled View entry button beside a text-only Related articles; over the second floats an extended action button, a pencil icon with Add entry.

The spec

As before, no Figma file: every prototype carries its own spec in a section of the page. At least spacing, font, font size and line height, the size of each button, and every other property that shapes it: roundness, stroke width, icon size, the state layers, the disabled look, how the button group joins. The numbers are read from the values the prototype runs on, so the spec cannot drift from the design.

Research

What a button is and how it behaves, from Apple's Buttons and Material 3's All buttons. Nothing about tone yet, only the button.

The parts

One container and what it holds. The emphasis comes from the container alone: fill, tint, outline or nothing.

  1. ContainerFill, tint, outline or nothing; this is what sets the emphasis
  2. IconOptional; before the label, 18 in a 40 button
  3. LabelA short verb; the one thing the button does
  4. State layerCovers the button on hover and press, in the label's colour
  5. OutlineOnly on the outlined style; gives a transparent button an edge

The emphasis ladder

Material names five common buttons, Apple four styles. They line up as one ladder: the more the container fills, the louder the button. Use the loudest only once per view.

Filled · primary

The one action the view is for: save, send, continue. Apple: Prominent.

Tonal · secondary

A softer fill for the action next in line. Apple: Bordered, a grey or tinted capsule.

Outlined · tertiary

Transparent with an edge: important, but not the main move. Material only.

Text · lowest

No container until hovered; for the many small actions in cards and dialogs. Apple: Borderless.

Destructive is a colour

Not a level: any style with a red label. In Apple's alert it is a grey capsule, not the blue one.

Same build, same size

Only the container changes; height, padding and label stay, so levels can sit side by side.

One build, every button

What a button holds is a choice apart from its emphasis. Mixing the two gives every button the brief asks for, from one build.

Icon and text

The icon leads; less padding on the icon side (16 against 24) so the pair looks centred.

Text only

The default. Even padding on both sides; the label sets the width.

Icon only

A square (or circle) as tall as the others. It still needs a name: a tooltip and an accessible label.

Button group, single selection

Joined buttons for one choice: picking one unpicks the last. Material: connected button group; Apple: segmented control.

Try it

Plain buttons with no tone, doing everything the brief asks. Change what they hold, turn them off, then point at one and press it. In the group, Tab lands once; the arrow keys move the pick.

Four states

Hover and press lay a thin layer of the label's colour over the button; disabled drains the colour out of both container and label. Focus, from the keyboard, is a ring on top of any of them.
