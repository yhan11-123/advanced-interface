Lists

Oct 19, 2026 lists system navigate expand research spec micro-motion tone

Brief

Two sets of list items, each expressing a tone. Tones can repeat. Every design has to do its job. Each one gets a drawing and a working prototype.

Tone is the soft attribute: the personality, the associations, the attitude. Function is the hard one: what the list is for and what it lets you do. A list's function is to be scanned and acted on, so the tone has to live in how the rows look, answer the pointer and move, without getting in the way of reading.

The rule that shapes everything: the list item works as a system. One basic structure, many configurations.

The brief's own drawing shows the system as three rows of one build: label only; an icon, label and supporting text, and trailing text; an avatar, two lines and a trailing icon. Every row keeps 16 at the sides, 12 between slots and 10 above and below. Beside it, an Apple grouped list: Title, Detail and a chevron, three times.

The spec

As before, no Figma file: every prototype carries its own spec in a section of the page. At least spacing, font, font size and line height, the row's size, and every other property that shapes it: slot sizes, gaps, roundness, stroke width, the state layers. The numbers are read from the values the prototype runs on, so the spec cannot drift from the design.

Research

Read against Apple's Lists and tables guidelines and Material 3's Lists, every tab: overview, specs, guidelines, accessibility.

One structure, three slots

Material describes a list item as three slots inside one container. Only the container and the label are required; everything else is optional. The content slot is always the widest, so the words keep their room whatever sits at either end.

  1. ContainerThe row. Its height is set by the tallest thing in it: Material uses 56, 72 or 88 dp.
  2. Leading slotIcon (20 in the brief, 24 dp in Material), avatar 40, image or video thumbnail, a selection control, or nothing.
  3. LabelThe only text that is required. Short, so it scans.
  4. Supporting textOptional, one to three lines, truncated when it runs over.
  5. Trailing slotText (a price, a count, a date), an icon or icon button, a control, or text and an icon together.
  6. DividerOptional; inset to the text, or full width. Material now prefers gaps for contained lists.

What each slot can hold

Leading: nothing, an icon, an image

Or an avatar for a person. Whatever it is, it sits at the leading edge in every row, so the eye runs straight down it.

Content: one line or two

The label alone, or with supporting text under it. The row grows to fit; the leading slot stays centred until the text runs to three lines.

Trailing: text, an icon, or both

Text for meta information. An icon for status or an action. Both, as in Apple's Title, Detail and chevron.

Two actions

The brief asks for both, and the two systems draw a clear line between them: one row leaves the list, the other stays and opens.

Three states, and focus

Material lays a state layer over the whole row in the content colour: about 8% on hover, 10% when pressed, with a ripple from the finger. Apple highlights a pressed row briefly; on a navigating list the chosen row keeps its highlight. Focus is not in the brief, but a keyboard has to see where it is.

Rules the tone has to survive

Same place, every row

Leading visuals and the label start at the same x in every row. Don't move elements from row to row; a list is read by running down it.

Short, and cut with care

Keep labels brief. When text has to be cut, a cut in the middle can keep both the start and the end readable.

Big enough to hit

Material's minimum target is 48 dp, Apple's 44 pt. A slim tone can draw a thin row, but the row it hit-tests stays tall.

One action per row

A navigating row is one target. Don't add a second button inside it, and don't use an info button to drill in; that is the chevron's job.

Never colour alone

A selected or open row needs a second cue: a check, a turned chevron, a change in weight. Not only a tint.

Gaps or dividers

Material now groups contained lists with gaps and rounded segments, and keeps dividers for open lists that need a firmer break.
