Menus

Oct 26, 2026 menus system shortcut submenu research spec tone

Brief

Two sets of menus, each expressing a tone. Tones can repeat. Every design has to do its job. Each one gets a drawing and a working prototype.

Tone is the soft attribute: the personality, the associations, the attitude. Function is the hard one: what the menu is for and what it lets you do. A menu's function is to offer a short list of commands, let you pick one, and get out of the way, so the tone has to live in how the items look, answer the pointer and open, without slowing the pick.

The rule that shapes everything: the menu item works as a system. One basic structure, many configurations.

The brief shows two drawings. Apple's: a dark menu on a warm gradient, three items with shortcuts right-aligned, a divider, and a Submenu item whose highlighted row opens a second menu beside it, Item X lit in red. Material's: one item build measured out, items 48 tall with 2 between, an icon and label, a trailing shortcut, a section label 32 tall, corners of 16 on the menu and 12 on the active item.

The spec

As before, no Figma file: every prototype carries its own spec in a section of the page. At least spacing, font, font size and line height, the size of the menu and its items, and every other property that shapes it: roundness, stroke width, shadow, the state layers, how the submenu opens. The numbers are read from the values the prototype runs on, so the spec cannot drift from the design.

Research

What a menu item is and how it behaves, from Apple's Menus and Material 3's Menus. Nothing about tone yet, only the menu.

The parts

One container, one item build. Only the container and the label are required.

  1. ContainerThe menu; as wide as its longest item
  2. Left iconOptional; the same column in every item
  3. LabelThe one required text; one line, a verb
  4. RightA shortcut, a badge, an icon, or a submenu chevron
  5. DividerSplits the menu into groups
  6. Section labelNames a group; not pickable

One build, many items

Each side has a few options. Mixing them gives every item the menu needs, from one build.

Left: nothing or an icon

When one item has an icon, the labels still start on one line.

Right: shortcut, icon, badge, chevron

One at a time. The chevron means the item opens a submenu.

Grouping: dividers and sections

Related commands sit together; a label names the group when it needs one.

Try it

A plain menu with no tone, doing everything the brief asks. Point at an item, press it, or use the arrow keys; Sort by opens its submenu to the side.

The keyboard's highlight looks the same as hover: in a menu, whichever item is lit is the one Return will pick.
