Toolbars

Nov 2, 2026 toolbars system alignment buttons research spec tone

Brief

Two sets of toolbars, each expressing a tone. Tones can repeat. Every design has to do its job. Each one gets a drawing and a working prototype.

Tone is the soft attribute: the personality, the associations, the attitude. Function is the hard one: what the toolbar is for and what it lets you do. A toolbar's function is to keep the few commands you use most within one reach of the content they act on, and to stay out of the content's way, so the tone has to live in how the bar sits, how its buttons look and how they answer the pointer, without hiding what each one does.

The rule that shapes everything: the toolbar works as a system. One basic structure, many configurations.

The brief shows two drawings. Apple's: the top of an iPad window, its toolbar split into three groups. Leading holds a back button and the title; center holds six tool icons in one capsule; trailing holds undo, share and more in a capsule, then compose on its own. Material's: a floating toolbar measured out, 64 tall, 8 of padding at each end, four buttons 4 apart; beside it, 8 away, a 56 primary action with its own rounded square.

The spec

As before, no Figma file: every prototype carries its own spec in a section of the page. At least spacing, font, font size and line height, the size of the bar and its buttons, and every other property that shapes it: roundness, stroke width, shadow, the state layers, how it changes between alignments. The numbers are read from the values the prototype runs on, so the spec cannot drift from the design.

Research

What a toolbar is and how it behaves, from Apple's Toolbars and Material 3's Toolbars. Nothing about tone yet, only the toolbar.

The parts

One container, one button build. A primary action can stand beside the bar.

  1. ContainerThe bar; as wide as its buttons, never the full screen when it floats
  2. Button48 square for an icon; wider for a label; the one build
  3. State layerCovers the button on hover and press
  4. Icon or labelAn icon, a label, or both, side by side
  5. Primary actionOptional; the one most used command, beside the bar

One build, many toolbars

Where the bar sits and what its buttons hold are two separate choices. Mixing them gives every toolbar the brief asks for, from one build.

Left or right

The bar holds an edge, next to the content or the hand it serves.

Center

The bar sits on the middle line; it grows out to both sides when it gains a button.

Icon, text, or both

Same button, same height. Only its width changes with what it holds.

Try it

A plain toolbar with no tone, doing everything the brief asks. Move it to a side, change what its buttons hold, then point at a button and press it. Tab lands on the bar once; the arrow keys move along it.

Three states

A tool that stays on (Draw, say) is a fourth look, not a fourth state: it still answers hover and press on top of being on.

Process

Ideation

Loose sketches, each running live in its box, each with every part the brief asks for: icon, text, and icon and text buttons in three states, and an align switch at the bottom right that moves the toolbar left, center or right. The first five come from one Touch Bar reference, split by the kind of row. Each has its own motion as well as its own look.

Deliverables

Two sets of toolbars, each a tone. Each set is one prototype: the toolbar in use, moving between left, center and right and between icon, text and icon and text buttons, then every configuration laid out in enabled, hover and pressed, then the spec.
