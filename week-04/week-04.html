Week 04

Sliders

Oct 12, 2026 sliders stepped continuous research spec micro-motion tone

Brief

Six components: three sets, each one stepped slider and one continuous slider, each set expressing a tone. Tones can repeat across sets. Every design has to do its job. Each one gets a drawing and a working prototype.

Tone is the soft attribute: the personality, the associations, the attitude. Function is the hard one: what the control is for and what it lets you do. Here the function is fixed, so the tone has to live in how the slider looks, moves and answers the finger.

One rule cuts across all six: every slider has to tell you the value it is set to, either while it is being dragged or as a label that stays.

What each prototype has to show

PicksValue shownStates

The brief's own examples show both routes. For stepped: Material's floating bubble reading “90” over a row of stop dots, and the macOS Energy Saver slider, labelled 1 min, 15 min, 1 hr, 3 hrs, Never. For continuous: Material's bubble reading “71”, and an Opacity slider with a 50% field and a stepper beside it.

The spec

Same as last week, no Figma file: every prototype carries its own spec in a section of the prototype page. At least spacing, font, font size and line height, the slider's size, and every other property that shapes it: track height, handle size, roundness, stroke width, stop size, the value label's size and offset. The numbers are read from the values the prototype runs on, so the spec cannot drift from the design.

Research

A slider picks a value by place instead of by typing. That makes it quick and rough. Everything the guidelines add is there to put back the precision that dragging takes away.

Read mainly against Apple's Sliders guidelines and Material 3's sliders, with Material's own token values. IBM's Carbon is the third voice again, and the WAI-ARIA slider pattern sets the keyboard. Where they agree, it is a rule. Where they differ, it is room for a tone.

Two sliders

Four ways to tell the value

The brief allows either route. The systems between them use four, and a tone can pick any of them, or combine two.

A bubble while dragging

Material: a pill in the inverse colour, 12 dp above the handle, there only while the handle is held. Above, so the finger cannot cover it.

Words on the stops

macOS: tick marks with labels under them. Label the ends, plus a few in between when the scale is not even, as in Energy Saver.

A field beside it

Apple and Carbon: the exact number in a text field, typed into when dragging is not precise enough. Apple adds a stepper for whole steps.

The result itself

Apple: give live feedback, like the Dock icons growing as the Size slider moves. The thing being changed becomes the label.

Slider, up close

Material's 2024 slider, drawn from its own token values in dp. The handle is a thin upright bar, not a dot, and it sits in a gap cut out of the track.

  1. Active track16 dp tall, fully round, in primary. The part between minimum and handle.
  2. Handle4 × 44 dp, fully round. Narrows to 2 dp when pressed or focused.
  3. Gap6 dp of nothing on each side of the handle, so the track never runs under it.
  4. Inactive track16 dp, secondary container. What is left to the maximum.
  5. Stop indicator4 dp dots. On a stepped slider, one per stop; on a continuous one, a single dot marks the end.
  6. Value indicatorA pill in inverse surface, Label Large type (14 sp on a 20 sp line), its bottom 12 dp above the handle.
  7. Target48 dp around the handle, though the drawing is only 4 dp wide.

The three states the brief asks for sit close together and are easy to blur. Hover says “you can grab this” before any touch, and only exists with a pointer. Pressed is the instant the handle is caught, before it has moved. Drag is the handle following the finger, and it is where the value has to be readable. Material narrows the handle on press, the opposite of last week's switch, which swelled: a thin bar hides less of the track under the finger.

Rules the tone has to survive

Small on the left, big on the right

Every system agrees: minimum on the leading side, maximum trailing; on a vertical slider, minimum at the bottom. A tone can redraw the track, never flip it.

Above the finger, not under it

NN/g: labels and the current value go above or beside the slider. Anything below the handle is under the hand while it drags.

Thin to look at, wide to catch

Material's handle is 4 dp drawn and 48 dp to touch; Apple asks 44 pt. The handle can be a hairline, the target cannot.

Few stops, all drawn

A stepped slider shows its choices before the drag. Past about ten stops it stops being stepped in practice; Apple suggests labelling only the ends then.

Works without dragging

Arrows move one step, Home and End jump to the ends, Page Up and Down move further. Focus sits on the handle, and the value is read out with it.

Say the word, not the index

When a stop is a word or a unit, aria-valuetext carries it. A screen reader should hear “15 minutes”, not “2”.

Rough by nature

NN/g: sliders suit values where close enough is fine. When the exact number matters, pair it with a field so it can be typed.

Still to read
